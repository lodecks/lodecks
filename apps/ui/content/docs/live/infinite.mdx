---
title: "Infinite mode"
description: "Any song, forever. An infinite jukebox on every deck."
---

Flip a deck to **infinite** and the song never ends. lodecks listens to the
track, finds the bars that sound alike, and keeps jumping between them on the
bar lines. The music keeps moving — it just never runs out, and never misses
a beat.

<Try code={`a.infinite()
a.play()
a.cue(7)   // drop 3: most of intro's arcs are from here on
a.show("graph")`} label="watch it branch" />

The graph draws the song as a circle of bars, with an arc for every pair
that sound alike. When the deck takes an arc, you see it.

## How it works

<Steps>
<Step>
### Listen
When a pack loads, its stems are analysed in the background, in about a
second: the timbre, the harmony and the loudness of every bar and beat.
</Step>
<Step>
### Connect
Bars that sound alike are joined by an arc. The graph is saved with the pack,
so a song is only analysed once.
</Step>
<Step>
### Walk
While the song plays, the deck may take an arc at the next bar line, landing
in the same place of a phrase elsewhere in the song. The longer it goes
without branching, the more likely it gets.
</Step>
<Step>
### Seam
Before a jump, each stem's level just after the boundary is compared at both
spots: jumps that would cut a vocal mid-word, or slam loud material into a
quiet moment, are skipped. The new spot starts exactly on the downbeat while
the old audio rings out for 120 ms.
</Step>
</Steps>

Stems and stem voices jump together and stay on the grid. Loops, oneshots
and effects are left alone.

## In code

```js
a.infinite()                 // on
a.infinite(false)            // off: the song plays straight
a.infinite("beat")           // branch on beats, like the classic jukebox
a.infinite("low")            // branch rarely, only to the closest matches
a.infinite("beat", "high")   // both
a.branch()                   // take an arc at the next boundary
```

| Option | |
| --- | --- |
| `"bar"` | branch on bar lines, at the same place of a 4-bar phrase (the default) |
| `"beat"` | branch on beats, at the same beat of the bar |
| `"low"` | branch rarely, only to the closest matches (the smoothest) |
| `"mid"` | the default |
| `"high"` | branch often, to looser matches |

It is off until you turn it on, and the setting belongs to the deck: it stays
when another pack loads.

<Try code={`a.infinite("high")
a.play()
a.cue(7)
a.show("graph")
every(4, () => a.branch())   // asks for a jump every 4 bars`} label="restless" />

A song only branches where it has arcs: some parts repeat a lot and are full
of them, others are one of a kind and have none. `branch()` waits for the
next bar that has one.

## With everything else

- **Loops hold it.** While a deck loops, it does not branch; letting go of
  the loop continues the walk.
- **Cue jumps** move the song, and the walk goes on from the new spot.
- **Taking over**: during `take()` and auto mix, the outgoing song may take
  one arc to reach a better place to leave from, and the deck stops
  branching while the blend plays.
- **Needs stems.** The analysis listens to the stems; a pack without them
  plays straight.

`deck().show("controls")` has the same switches as buttons: **∞**, the unit
(**bar** / **beat**), **jump** and the variety (**low** / **mid** / **high**).

## Where it comes from

The idea is Paul Lamere's [Infinite Jukebox](https://musicmachinery.com/2012/11/12/the-infinite-jukebox/)
(2012), which made any song on the web play forever. The branching in lodecks
is ported from rigdern's [InfiniteJukeboxAlgorithm](https://github.com/rigdern/InfiniteJukeboxAlgorithm)
(MIT), itself derived from UnderMybrella's Eternal Jukebox. Where those used
Spotify's audio analysis, lodecks computes its own features from the stems,
on the pack's own grid, and branches on bars as well as beats, which suits a
DJ set.
